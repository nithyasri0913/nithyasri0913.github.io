Autonomous Drone Racing with RL and Sim-to-Real Transfer

ESE 6510, University of Pennsylvania • Spring 2026

In ESE 6510 we trained a quadrotor to race through gates using reinforcement learning. We trained policies entirely in simulation in NVIDIA Isaac Lab, then flew them on a real drone through a ROS 2 pipeline. The video above shows the policy in sim.

Training in Simulation

We trained the racing policies with PPO on a powerloop track, running many simulated drones in parallel in Isaac Lab. The policy maps the drone's state directly to control commands, so there is no separate planner or trajectory to track.

Reward Design

Most of the work was in shaping the reward. We combined dense terms that guide the drone toward the next gate with a sparse bonus for passing each gate and penalties for crashing:

  • Gate pass: a large bonus each time the drone passes through a gate
  • Progress: rewards getting closer to the next gate
  • Velocity toward gate: rewards flying fast in the right direction
  • Centering: rewards lining up with the center of the gate
  • Escape: helps the drone climb out of the altitude local optimum (below)
  • Crash penalty and death cost: penalize collisions and ending the episode early

Most of the tuning went into balancing these terms: push too hard for speed and the drone crashes, penalize crashes too hard and it learns to fly cautiously and slowly.

On a 7-gate track with 2.0 m obstacles, the policy got stuck in an altitude local optimum and never made it past the obstacles. A gate-centric vertical progress reward got it out, and after that the drone flew through all the gates consistently.

Getting It to Fly for Real

A policy that flies well in sim can still fail on hardware. To make ours robust, we trained with domain randomization over:

  • Aerodynamic drag
  • PID controller gains
  • Thrust-to-weight ratio

On the real drone, the policy completed three racing laps in 17.14 s.

When real flights went wrong, we went through the ROS 2 bag recordings to find out why. The main problems were latency mismatches between perception and control, and policy instability. Those findings decided what we debugged next.
