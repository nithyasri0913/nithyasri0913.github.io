F1TENTH Autonomous Racing

ESE 6150, University of Pennsylvania • Fall 2026

ESE 6150 (F1TENTH, now called RoboRacer) is Penn's autonomous racing course. Each team gets a 1/10-scale race car with a LiDAR and an onboard computer, and over the semester we build the full autonomy stack ourselves in ROS 2: mapping, localization, planning and control. There are three races: Race 1, Race 2 and a final race. Each one adds new methods and pushes for faster laps.

The first labs covered reactive navigation, such as wall following and follow-the-gap, where the car reacts directly to what the LiDAR sees and has no map. The races go further: build a map of the track, know where you are on it, and follow the fastest line you can. This page is where I'm writing up how our car works, what went wrong, and what we're trying next.

Race 1: Pure Pursuit

For the first race we implemented pure pursuit in C++ on ROS 2 (Jazzy). The pipeline:

  • Build a map of the track with SLAM.
  • Localize on that map with a particle filter.
  • Compute minimum-curvature and minimum-time racelines as waypoints.
  • Track the raceline with pure pursuit and a velocity-dependent lookahead.

On the real car this averaged 8.7 s per lap on a ~20 m track, and we placed third in the class.

What's Next

For Race 2 and the final race we're implementing LQR, MPC and MPPI.
